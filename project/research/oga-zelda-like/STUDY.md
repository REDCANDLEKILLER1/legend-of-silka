  on what (if anything) gets fitted into THE LEGEND OF SILKA.

## What's in it
- **Overworld.png (640×576, 16px grid)** — the main sheet: water + waterfall,
  two plank houses, hedges/bushes, fences, striated earth cliffs with grass
  lips, a big shingled roof piece, white-stone castle pieces (tower, gate
  arch, banners, a small statue), a market stall, produce crates, barrels,
  gravestones, bones — and **three round stone fountains (~48×48 each) that
  are one fountain in 3 animation frames**: circular basin, blue water, a
  white jet in the middle. This is the "use more squares" lesson in one
  object: a fountain is a 3×3-tile composition, never one tile.
- **objects.png** — hearts, chests, pots, fire frames, slash effects, rocks,
  speech bubbles, crop/plant growth stages, UI bits.
- **Inner.png / cave.png** — interior + cave tiles (stone floors, doors).
- **character.png / NPC_test.png** — a Link-like hero + NPCs. NOT candidates:
  the game's cast is Michael's canon, drawn our way.
- **font.png** — a pixel font. Not a candidate (our UI text is our own).

## Honest flags
- One commenter on the page suspects the trees/bushes echo Minish Cap too
  closely. The license is CC0 regardless; the practical risk to a free game
  is a takedown, not damages (Michael: "We're not monetizing this"). Style
  note stands on its own: the round hedge-balls are the most MC-flavoured
  pieces in the set.
- Palette: softer and brighter (GBA-flavoured) than our punchier SNES
  ramps. Anything fitted needs a palette pass to sit in our world — same
  fit-authority treatment as every other pack.

## Fitting candidates (ranked)
1. **The fountain** — the plaza could carry a real 3-frame fountain by the
   monument, or the fields' crossroads could. Directly answers Michael's
   "that's not a fountain" — this is what one looks like in tiles.
2. **Cliff/earth tiles** — striated earth with grass lips for the fields'
   stone patch and the cave mouth approach.
3. **Market stall + produce crates** — dressing for the general store corner.
4. **Hedges, fences, gravestones** — village/fields dressing; the gravestones
   could serve the chapel corner.
