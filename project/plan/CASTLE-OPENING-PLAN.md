## The shape of it

The game opens inside the incident, a hundred harvests before Corn Man.
Three acts: the feast, the taking, the flinch — then the time-pass, and the
game hands you the boy. The model is A Link to the Past's castle opening
more than Zelda II's: Zelda II *shows* you a sleeping princess on one
screen; ALttP puts you on your feet inside the castle while the crisis is
still happening, and the castle teaches you to walk before it teaches you
anything else.

## The beats

1. **The hall, at peace.** The harvest feast in the King's hall, the night
   before Poppy's wedding ride down the Green Seam. Long tables, the
   valley's best, the King at the head. This beat has one job: make the
   player love the room before it breaks.
2. **Talk of the secret.** Somewhere in the feast talk, it's planted
   plainly: the Golden Cob of Courage sleeps in the Great Barn, and only
   Poppy knows its resting place. The player hears it as gossip, not as a
   quest marker.
3. **The crows first.** Before the Wizard, his weather: crows settling on
   the hall's beams, the candles bending, the music thinning. The game
   already has crows as the Blight King's watchers — this is where that
   starts.
4. **The arrival and the demand.** The Storm Wizard in the doorway. He
   doesn't fight the room; he asks. The secret, for the Blight King.
5. **The refusal.** Poppy stands and will not tell. This is her beat — the
   strongest moment in the opening, and it has to be hers, not the King's.
6. **The sleep and the taking.** He lays the endless sleep on her where she
   stands — and then he takes her. Gone through the storm-door with the
   crows, while the room is still reaching for its feet. Sleep *and*
   kidnapping, both, in one motion.
7. **The King's last strength.** The old King goes to the Great Barn and
   seals it — the binding force, the eight empty sockets willed into
   being — and spends himself doing it. He does not come back to the hall.
8. **The land flinches.** Outside, that same night: Mount Shuck shucked
   bare in one hard pull, the mountain cracking and the Silk River born,
   running down to widen the pond into Ambermere. The valley sets wrong,
   like a fence poured in the rain.
9. **A hundred harvests later.** The time-pass. Control hands to the player
   as Corn Man walks in — and the incident becomes the game.

## The big open question: whose feet are you on during the incident?

Three honest options:

- **A. You play young Pearl.** She was a girl in that hall — she saw it,
  she lived, she's the one telling it a hundred years later over Corn
  Man's marked hand. You walk her through the feast, talk to the guests,
  stand close when it breaks. She can't stop any of it, which is exactly
  right — a witness isn't supposed to. And it welds the opening to the
  game's existing frame: old Pearl's telling *is* the flashback, and when
  the tale ends, you're holding the boy's hand with the golden kernel on
  it. Muse's lean is here.
- **B. You play Poppy.** You walk her through her own feast, and the
  refusal is your last act before control is taken from you — the
  kidnapping lands harder because the hands it happens to were yours a
  second ago. Stronger gut-punch, but it spends the princess as a playable
  character in minute one, and the refusal scene then needs her playable
  walk to end on rails anyway.
- **C. Nobody — a staged telling.** Pearl narrates over still tableaux,
  Zelda II style, and the first feet on the ground are Corn Man's, a
  hundred harvests later. Shortest, cheapest, safest — and the least
  alive. The castle becomes a picture instead of a place.

## The other decisions waiting on Michael

- **Where is she held?** This drives what the endgame means. Cleanest fit
  with what exists: the Wizard bore her east, into the storm's own country
  across the Silk River — which is why the barns run east-first and why
  the trail bends toward the Great Barn, where he makes his last stand
  over her, because the Golden Cob is the only thing that can wake her and
  he means to control the waking. Alternative: she's sealed *inside* the
  Great Barn with the Cob, and the King's seal is both her prison and her
  protection. Either works; they make different final rooms.
- **Where does Corn Man walk in?** Into the ruined hall itself, a hundred
  years on — the castle as the game's starting ground, with Home Farm
  beyond its gate? Or the castle is prologue-only, and he walks into Home
  Farm as he does now, with Eli waiting? The first makes the castle a
  place you return to; the second keeps it a memory.
- **Where does the castle sit on the land?** It's not on the v44 sheet.
  Near the Great Barn's hill? North, toward the hill country Poppy was to
  ride down from? The lore gives her a bridal road from the hills through
  the Seam to the Great Barn — the castle wants a home at one end of that
  road.
- **How does the castle teach?** If the opening is playable, the hall
  teaches walk and talk — which means Eli's road lessons on Home Farm
  should hand off, not repeat. One teacher per skill: the hall teaches
  feet and voices; Eli teaches the road and the wrench.
- **The King's death — shown or stated?** Draft says stated, not shown: he
  goes out to the sealing and the hall dims behind him, and Pearl's line
  carries the death. Michael's stories are heroic, not grim — but it's his
  call, it's his scene.
- **The flinch — how shown?** The game can already paint its own world
  map. The flinch could play as the map itself changing: green mountain,
  then bare rings; no river, then the Silk River running. Before and
  after, in the land's own picture.
