## The fusion, in one line

Zelda II's systems — life and magic meters, spells taught by
people, experience that levels three tracks, side-view combat with
a high/low guard — carried on Zelda I's bones: the top-down
overworld, heart-style growth, and the item-get moment.

## Health

- The HUD carries a LIFE meter of blocks (Zelda II style), red.
  CornMan starts with 4 blocks.
- **Life Vessels** raise the maximum, one block each, to 8 — found
  in palaces, given for quests, hidden in the world (the Zelda I
  heart-container beat, in Zelda II's meter).
- Damage is a **table, not a formula** (Zelda II's true design,
  verified in RESEARCH-ZELDA-HEALTH.md): every enemy carries a
  damage class, and a hit reads class × his LIFE level off an 8×8
  table. The weakest class falls from a full block at LIFE 1 to a
  quarter-block at LIFE 8; the top class takes 255 at *every*
  level — the instant-death class. **The Wizard's purple fire is
  that class.** It never gets cheaper, at any level, ever.
- **Lives:** he starts with 3. Falling spends one and wakes him at
  the last door he entered (side view) or the village edge
  (overworld). Losing the last life is a game over: he wakes at the
  Corn Palace, keeping every level, spell, and item — the world
  does not take his growth back — **but every unspent experience
  point is lost** (Zelda II's one sting, and what makes banking
  points a gamble). Little CornMan dolls hidden in the world each
  add a life (Zelda II's Link dolls, ours).

## Magic

- A MAGIC meter sits under LIFE, golden — gold is the kingdom's
  magic color, the same gold as the pit chains' glow. Starts at 4
  blocks; **Magic Vessels** raise it to 8, same as life.
- Every spell costs magic; the MAGIC level lowers the cost.
- Restoration is **people, split in two** (Zelda II's town law):
  Winnie restores LIFE at the inn; Father Corin restores MAGIC at
  the temple. Rest is a place and a person, not a menu.

### Spells — taught by people, never found in a box

Zelda II's law: a wise man in a town teaches each spell, some only
after a kindness is done. Ours are taught by the cast he already
knows:

| Spell | What it does | Teacher |
|---|---|---|
| MEND | Restores exactly 3 blocks of life — healing is rationed, never a full heal (Zelda II's LIFE) | Winnie, at the inn |
| HUSK | A golden husk of light; halves all damage until it breaks | Father Corin, at the temple |
| EMBER | Golden fire from the wrench's head (Zelda II's FIRE) | Brand, at the forge |
| LEAP | A jump twice his height, for side-view heights (Zelda II's JUMP) | Vesper, at the tower |
| TURN | The wrench turned flat reflects spells back at their caster (Zelda II's REFLECT) | Father Corin, later |
| THUNDER | The sky answers: every enemy on screen falls. Costly. Late game (Zelda II's THUNDER) | OPEN |

FAIRY and SPELL from Zelda II are held in reserve — no Silka form
proposed yet.

## Techniques — free, taught, no magic cost

- **SPIN** — the warden's spin, taught by the garrison captain in
  the courtyard during the Day, before the descent (the wrench
  quest). The answer to being surrounded.
- **DOWN SLAM** — in side view, strike downward in the air onto
  what is below (Zelda II's Down Thrust). Teacher: OPEN (a guard
  in Part Two's village?).
- **UP THRUST** — strike upward at what hangs or flies above.
  Teacher: OPEN.

## Growth — experience, three tracks

Enemies and deeds earn experience. At each threshold he chooses
one track to raise (Zelda II's law — the choice is the player's),
or **cancels** and keeps banking toward a dearer track:

- **ATTACK** — the wrench hits harder.
- **MAGIC** — every spell costs less.
- **LIFE** — every hit he takes costs him less.

Two mercies ride on the system (both verified Zelda II law):
**raising LIFE refills the life meter to full, and raising MAGIC
refills the magic meter** — a level chosen at the right moment is
a potion. And **securing a palace's Emerald tops his experience
up to the next threshold automatically** — Zelda II's crystal
rule wearing our canon: every Emerald made safe guarantees a
level.

## Combat shape (side view, Zelda II)

- The wrench strikes where he aims: high or low. His guard is the
  same — he can block high or low with the wrench's flat; what he
  cannot do is block both at once.
- The spin covers all around him but leaves him open the moment
  after — power with a price, or it is the only move anyone uses.
