## The shape of the whole game

Part One opens with a dream: CornMan falls into a village he has never
seen, and strangers carry him to their inn. He wakes in his own bed at
the castle — it was only a dream — and plays out his last normal day.
That night the attack makes the dream start coming true, and the King
is taken. Part Two opens with the fall again — this time for real, and
this time the player plays the village the dream only showed him.

The dream is not a trick and not a bad omen hanging over the game. It
is a warning he doesn't understand yet. When he falls for real, the
player already knows every face in that village — and none of them
know him. He dreamed them before he ever met them.

## PART ONE — THE DREAM AND THE DAY

### Scene 1 — The Dream (the fall)

*Night. A storm over a village CornMan has never seen. Something falls
out of the sky — CornMan — down the village path. The inn lady and a
soldier find him where he landed.*

- SOLDIER: "They're coming!"
- INN LADY: "Who is this?"
- SOLDIER: "I don't know."
- INN LADY: "We have to take him in!"
- INN LADY: "Quick! To the inn!"

*They pick him up and carry him through the rain to the inn.*

> Status: BUILT. This is the Scene 1 act, exactly as it plays in the
> game file — full screen, storm, carry and all. It is a movie, not
> played. The dream ends as they carry him into the inn.

### Scene 2 — The Wake

*The dream's last image is CornMan in the inn's bed. The wake opens
on the same bed, in the same framing — but it is his own bed, in his
own house (the west cottage by the mill). A match-cut on the bed:
same bed, new room, morning.*

CornMan sits up.

- CORNMAN: "That was a terrible dream."

*He looks around his own room.*

- CORNMAN: "I'm home."

*Then he sees the light — and remembers what morning it is.*

- CORNMAN: "Oh no — I'm late!"

> laid over the wake scene, so the two beds are one bed on screen
> and the cut is smooth.
>
> Superseded: the old Scene 1 plan had him waking at midnight in
> the inn room, and an earlier draft of this script woke him in a
> castle bed chamber. Both are gone — he wakes at home, and he is
> late for the palace.

### Scene 3 — Good Morning, Kernal Corn

*An act inside the play. CornMan bursts out of his house on his own
(the exit plays itself), and the player walks him south down the
sidewalk, past the fountain, north toward the palace door. The
whole village is awake, and everybody loves him — good mornings and
waves the whole way. He can stop and talk to anyone he wants.*

*Almost at the palace door, the Child runs up and stops him. From
here the scene plays itself: control locks — the player holds
CornMan there and cannot move him until the talk is done.*

The Child snaps to attention and salutes, like a little soldier.

- CHILD: "Good morning, Kernal Corn!"
- CHILD: "I want to be like you one day!"
- CHILD: "Tell me the story again — how you captured the Wizard,
  Kernal Corn!"

CornMan is kind — and in a hurry. He is late, and he is supposed to
be at the palace right now.

- CORNMAN: "I can't talk right now, son — I'm late."
- CHILD: "Then at least tell me about the golden chains — and how
  they'll never break in a million billion years!"
- CORNMAN: "I'll talk to you soon."

*He shoos him gently — never unkind — and the Child steps out of
the way. Control returns to the player, and CornMan goes in the
palace door.*

> Michael's scene, set 2026-11-25 (dialogue drafted from his words,
> his to approve). Its job: in the first minutes of play, the player
> learns the whole backstory from a child's mouth — CornMan himself
> captured the Wizard, and the Wizard is held in the pit by golden
> chains of magic that will never break. The child says "never" the
> way children do. The Night will test it.

### Scene 4 — The Day

*Kernal Corn's normal day — the last one. He is King Cob's Kernal,
and tomorrow is the festival. The player walks the castle and the
village, meets everyone in their regular parts, and learns who
CornMan is by living his day: who he is to the King, to the guards,
to the village.*

The village cast is introduced here in their everyday lives — the
inn lady at her inn, the soldier at his post, the townspeople at
their work. Regular parts only. Nothing burns yet. The player has
seen these faces before — in the dream — and that is the point.

#### The castle, filled

The castle is a palace — people live here, and the Day is when the
player learns the place by walking it.

- **The garrison.** Soldiers fill the castle — posts at the doors,
  the towers, the stairs. They all look the same; that's fine, that's
  what a garrison is. Their real purpose is the dungeon (below).
- **The King.** King Cob holds his throne. He has a bed chamber of
  his own among the bedrooms — we may never need to show him in it;
  the throne is where the kingdom sees him, and the throne room is
  where the Night takes him.
- **The three other houses.** The palace's other residences have
  people in them — we have to decide who lives in each. OPEN.
- **The Priest.** The temple has a priest. Who he is: OPEN.
- **The Astrologer.** At the top of the tower lives the sky-watcher
  — not a wizard: an astrologer, a man who reads the sky. If anyone
  in the castle could have seen the storm coming, it's him.

#### The dungeon — the Wizard in the pit

Under the castle is the dungeon, and in the dungeon is the pit, and
in the pit is **the Wizard** — held there a long time. Everybody in
the castle knows he's down there. The garrison exists to keep him
there.

He is not held by iron. He is held by **magic**: golden chains,
beautiful as jewelry, glowing softly with the spell on them —
chains that will never break in a million billion years. (The
Child's words, Scene 3. The whole kingdom believes them.)

And it was **CornMan himself who captured him** — the kingdom's
favorite story, the one every child wants told again. That is why
the watch is his:

**Kernal Corn's primary job is to make sure the Wizard never
escapes.**

In the beginning we never see him. No face, no voice — just the pit,
the guards, and the knowledge. The player walks the Day knowing
something is locked under the castle.

### Scene 5 — The Night It Starts

*Midnight. A calm night — water moving, lamps flickering. Then a
large BOOM, and screams. The village is burning.*

CornMan runs through the burning village to the palace — the run
plays on the village map — and the confrontation plays inside, on the
castle-interior map, where the guards are already down.

The Storm Wizard stands before King Cob. He has come for the Corn
Emerald.

- STORM WIZARD: "Give me the Corn Emerald, King Cob — or I will

CornMan attacks — and is knocked down easily. The Wizard walks slowly

**King Cob is kidnapped. The emerald is NOT taken that night.**

## PART TWO — THE DREAM COMES TRUE

### Scene 6 — The Fall, For Real

Part Two opens exactly where the dream opened: the storm, the
village, CornMan falling out of the sky. The player has watched this
once. This time it is real, and this time he lands on his feet in a
village under attack — and the game hands him the controls.

### Scene 7 — The Village, For Real

The cast he dreamed is here, in their regular parts — the inn lady,
the soldier, the townspeople — except now their home is burning, and
their bigger parts begin: **the villagers fight the fires.** Buckets
from the well and the river, carried to the doors — the nearest free
hands take the nearest fire. CornMan fights beside people who have
never seen him before, in a village he already knows by heart.

From here the quest opens: every king and every place holds an
Emerald, and the Storm Wizard is gathering them all for the Evil
King. If he gets them all, they will rule. CornMan goes to war for
the Corn Emerald, for King Cob, and for Princess Silka.

## The cast

- **CornMan** — the hero. King Cob's Kernal ("Kernal Corn"). His
  weapon is a wrench. The C on his chest is his emblem — he carries
  no shield.
- **King Cob** — King of the corn kingdom. Kidnapped in Part One.
  Holder of the Corn Emerald.
- **Princess Silka** — her golden hair is corn silk. (Her scenes are
  to be written.)
- **The Storm Wizard** — faceless pointed hood, red eyes, purple
  fire. Gathering the Emeralds for the Evil King.
- **The Evil King** — the power behind the Wizard. Offstage so far.
- **Winnie, the Inn Lady** — keeps the village inn. In the dream
  she takes CornMan in; in the real village she is the heart of it.
- **The Soldier** — village guard. First to reach the fall, in the
  dream and in truth.
- **Rowe, Bucket Man** — villager; first link of the bucket chain.
- **Amber, Bucket Woman** — villager; second link of the bucket
  chain. "We have to take him in!"
- **Brand, the Smith** — the village smith.
- **Silas, the Elder** — village elder; the panic's voice.
- **Vesper, the Astrologer** — the castle tower's sky-watcher.
  Reads the stars, not spells.
- **Father Corin, the Priest** — keeper of the temple. Unwritten
  so far.
- **The garrison** — the castle's soldiers, one face many times.
  Their watch is the dungeon.
- **The Wizard in the pit** — see Scene 4. If the Storm Wizard and
  the prisoner are one and the same: OPEN, and the Night answers it.
- **Nib, the Child** — the village child (shelf 26), CornMan's
  smallest soldier. His salute and his questions open the Day

## Standing facts (canon — do not contradict)

- **His rank is spelled KERNAL** — K-E-R-N-A-L. Kernal Corn, King
  Cob's Kernal. In every line, every file, always. (Michael's
  spelling, set 2026-11-25. Not "Colonel," not "Kernel.")
- **There are no humans in this world. Everybody is corn folk** —
  every face in the game is a corn face, from the King to the
  smallest child. (Michael's ruling, 2026-11-25. It reversed an art
  direction the same night: the human-faced Astrologer board was
  superseded by his corn-faced one — "the guy with the purple robe
  is beautiful.")
- Every king and every place holds an **Emerald**. The Storm Wizard
  gathers them for the **Evil King**. If he gets them all, they rule.
- The Wizard's fire is **purple**.
- The Wizard in the pit is held by **golden chains of magic** —
  golden like jewelry, glowing with the spell. Not iron. They will
  never break in a million billion years.
- **CornMan captured the Wizard himself.** It is the kingdom's
  favorite story.
- CornMan's house is the west cottage by the mill, in the village.
  Part One's waking happens there — he is late for the palace.
- The castle: grand stairs reach only their own floor's balcony;
  floors connect only through the tower staircases; the castle exit
  is the ground-floor main doors only. There is no door out of the
  second floor.
- The dream (Scene 1) and the fall (Scene 6) are the same event,
  seen twice — once dreamed, once real.
